### 0.2 Sources searched
- **arXiv API** (`export.arxiv.org/api/query`). It returns the v1 `<published>` date, journal_ref and comment for each hit.
- **Web search**, used like Google Scholar for venue confirmation and discovering papers.
- **GitHub:** awesome lists (snowball), READMEs, raw LICENSE files, and the GitHub API where not rate-limited.
- **Hugging Face Hub API:** dataset and model cards, license tags, and `usedStorage`.
- **Project pages, venue proceedings and OpenReview:** ICLR, NeurIPS, CVF, PMLR, ACL Anthology, roboticsproceedings.org.
- **Official docs:** PX4, OpenVINO, ONNX Runtime, TensorRT, vLLM, LeRobot, PyTorch, Intel ARK.
- **Full-text reading:** for the 28 A/B candidate papers, the arXiv PDF was downloaded and converted with `pdftotext`. Quotes were taken from that text, not from summaries.

### 0.3 Inclusion and exclusion criteria
- **Topic (A/B).** Included: language-conditioned UAV or quadrotor control, navigation, mission planning, racing, aerial manipulation, and aerial VLN, dialog or object-goal datasets and benchmarks. Excluded:
  - communications or networking papers that only mention UAVs or LLMs
  - geo-localization and cross-view retrieval
  - remote-sensing VQA
  - swarm or agentic frameworks without language-conditioned control or a navigation benchmark
  - security benchmarks for LLM agents
  Surveys were screened but not tabulated.
- **Prioritization among on-topic hits (depth over breadth).** A paper was more likely to be tabulated if it:
  1. contributes a model together with a dataset, benchmark or simulator, or
  2. is peer-reviewed, or
  3. has released code or data, or
  4. represents a distinct sub-area (racing, manipulation, mission planning, onboard deployment, classic language-to-quadcopter).

  Method-only papers evaluated on existing aerial VLN benchmarks were screened and judged on-topic but not tabulated. Examples: CityNavAgent (ACL 2025), STMR, LongFly, OnFly, Fly0, AeroDuo (ACM MM 2025), SkyVLN (IROS 2025), NavAgent, History-Enhanced Two-Stage Transformer, FreeFly-Thinking, ViSA, OpenVLN, LogisticsVLN, UAV-VLN (ECMR 2025), GeoNav (Pattern Recognition 2026), NavFoM, AirHunt, APEX, AION, USS-Nav. DroneVLA (HRI 2026 LBR, open-loop validation only) was read in full and not tabulated.
- **Date rule.**
  - arXiv-only papers whose v1 was posted on or after 2026-04-01 are excluded and listed by URL in §8.
  - Reading of the rule: the constraints note says "2026년 4월 이후"; I read this as including April. The English task wording ("after April") would admit April papers, so papers with v1 in April 2026 are listed separately in §8 for the user to override.
  - Peer-reviewed versions are allowed regardless of date. One post-cutoff paper is included under this exception: SatNav (B11), whose NeurIPS 2026 acceptance appears on its fetched project page. Other post-cutoff papers that claim acceptance in an arXiv comment are listed under B but not tabulated.
- **Tools.** Current versions are allowed. Each tool row records the version and the check date (2026-10-04).

### F. Methodology facts (one line each)
- **Success thresholds.**
  - R2R counts a success when navigation error is <3 m (F7).
  - VLN-CE uses SUCCESS_DISTANCE 3.0 m with 0.25 m forward / 15° turn actions (F11).
  - Habitat PointNav requires stopping within 0.2 m (F13).
  - AerialVLN uses 20 m (F24).
- **Metric definitions.**
  - SR = 1[NE ≤ d_th].
  - OSR = 1[minimum distance from the path to the goal ≤ d_th].
  - SPL = S·l/max(p,l). It depends only on the endpoint.
  - nDTW and SDTW measure path fidelity (F8, F9).
- **Typical seen→unseen SR drops.** R2R Seq2Seq 38.6 → 21.8 (F7); VLN-CE Seq2Seq 0.25 → 0.20 (F11); AerialVLN Seq2Seq 2.9 → 1.1 (F24).
- **Language-grounding pitfalls.**
  - In R2R, a language-only model beats the full model on unseen environments, 22.1 vs 19.6 (F10).
  - In VLN-CE, removing the instruction only drops val-unseen SR from 0.20 to 0.17 (F11).
  - LIBERO VLAs keep about 0.9+ success under paraphrase but drop to 0.00–0.01 when the task or target is changed (F16, F17).
- **Saturation and overlap.**
  - LIBERO test tasks mirror training tasks: "Each test task τ′∈𝒯test mirrors a training task τ∈𝒯train" (F16).
  - OpenVLA-OFT averages 97.1% on LIBERO (F15).
- **Open-loop vs closed-loop.**
  - Validation MSE correlates weakly with real success: Pearson r 0.308 (F1).
  - The best-validation-loss checkpoint is "50 to 100% worse" (F2).
  - Driving open-loop metrics are dominated by ego status (F3, F4, F5).
- **Statistics.**
  - About 50 rollouts gives a CI width of about 20–30% absolute (F23).
  - Seed variance alone can produce "significant" differences (Henderson).
  - Report interval estimates (Agarwal).
  - Define success unambiguously (F21).

## 7. Bucket G. Gaps stated by authors

How to read this section: every quote is verbatim from the paper's own text. I extracted the arXiv PDFs to text locally, or used the fetched project page where marked. I grouped statements under recurring themes. A theme appears only if at least one author states it; there are no inferred gaps. The IDs refer to the rows above.

### G1. Instruction style and the role of language
- **AutoFly (A5).** Existing UAV VLN "relies on detailed, pre-specified instructions to guide the UAV along predetermined routes". It names two "fundamental limitations" of existing VLN datasets: "(1) over-reliance on explicit instruction-following rather than autonomous decision-making, and (2) insufficient real-world data representation, creating a significant sim-to-real gap."
- **AerialVLA (A7).** "A major limitation of existing UAV-VLN methods (e.g., TravelUAV [34]) is their reliance on dense, step-by-step oracle guidance derived from pre-recorded optimal trajectories." Also: "benchmarks such as TravelUAV [34] and recent methods [12, 39] typically inject dense, ground-truth-derived directional hints (e.g., 'Turn Right') directly into the input prompts." This is also an evaluation pitfall; see F.
- **UAV-ON (B6).** "most existing research follows the Vision-and-Language Navigation (VLN) paradigm, which heavily depends on sequential linguistic instructions, limiting its scalability and autonomy."
- **SINGER (A9).** "Directions for future work include policy conditioning on more verbose queries, maneuvers, environmental interaction, and responsiveness to dynamic environments."
- **SPF (A10).** "Performance can be sensitive to prompt phrasing."

### G2. Action space realism (discrete vs continuous, teleport vs physics)
- **TravelUAV (B5).** "Recent UAV VLN benchmarks ... typically adopt ground-based VLN settings, relying on a fixed set of discrete actions." Also: "UAV trajectories are inherently continuous and difficult to decompose into discrete actions, leading to unrealistic navigation when such simplifications are applied."
- **UAV-ON (B6).** "Existing aerial VLN approaches often rely on discrete waypoint execution or teleport-based movement, thus bypassing critical aspects of real-world navigation, such as obstacle avoidance, motion continuity, and collision handling".
- **AirNav (B8), §7.2.** The discrete action set "cannot fully represent the fine-grained motions and dynamic constraints involved in real UAV continuous control ... discrete actions may introduce trajectory approximation errors".
- **π, But Make It Fly (A15).** Transferring VLAs to aerial platforms "remains an open challenge due to the fundamental mismatch between the quasi-static dynamics of fixed-base arms and the underactuated, highly dynamic nature of flight."

### G3. Safety and collision avoidance
- **SINGER (A9).** "we also recognize collision avoidance as a new challenge introduced by deployment in the open-world and highlight this as an impactful open area for future work."
- **VLA-AN (A6).** Lists "safety issues with generative action policies" among "four major limitations of existing large aerial navigation models".
- **AIR-VLA (A14).** "addressing safety constraints in Aerial Manipulation Systems remains a pivotal direction for future research."
- **HUGE-Bench (B10).** VLA models show "substantial gaps in high-level semantic stage completion and safe execution".

### G4. Memory, long horizon, stopping
- **AerialVLA (A7).** "by prioritizing instantaneous reactive control over explicit historical memory, the agent occasionally faces challenges with global backtracking in highly repetitive urban structures". It also says "future work will integrate lightweight memory mechanisms for global reasoning and explore reinforcement learning fine-tuning to enable active exploration beyond static expert demonstrations."
- **Flex (A12).** "The framework presented in this manuscript is limited to instantaneous decisions. Indeed, the policy can only act with information from the current image and has no access to a history of representations or actions."
- **UAV-Flow (B7).** "Integrating fine-grained short-range execution with long-horizon planning is a key challenge ... and will be a major direction for future research."
- **UAV-ON (B6).** Results "expose limitations in termination mechanisms, especially for LLM-based agents operating in a zero-shot regime."
- **SatNav (B11), project page.** "the OS-SR gap across all models highlights missed stopping opportunities"
- **π, But Make It Fly (A15).** "fundamental limitations remain for long-horizon compositional manipulation and out-of-distribution adaptation".

### G5. Generalization (unseen instructions, environments, regions)
- **GRaD-Nav++ (A8).** "Our policy adapts to novel combinations of sub-tasks seen individually during training ... but fails on instructions composed entirely of unseen sub-tasks."
- **RaceVLA (A2).** "Future work will focus on improvement of visual and physical generalization in more unpredictable and diverse racing scenarios."
- **AerialVLA (A7).** "bounded by the nature of behavior cloning, the policy acts conservatively in extreme out-of-distribution scenarios".
- **AirNav (B8), §7.1.** "the generalization ability of models across different cities, countries, and seasonal conditions remains to be validated with more diverse data."
- **SatNav (B11), project page.** "Generalization remains challenging".
- **AerialVLN (B1).** "there is still a significant gap between the baseline model and human performance". The human SR is "∼80%".

### G6. Sim-to-real and real-world evaluation
- **TravelUAV (B5).** Future directions: "enhance the autonomous navigation capabilities of UAVs, enabling them to operate effectively in complex environments with minimal guidance" and "improve the transferability from UAV simulation to real-world deployment".
- **FlightGPT (A11).** "the system's performance, stability, and robustness in real-world settings remain unverified and call for further field testing and validation."
- **AirNav (B8), §7.3.** "differences between simulation and real UAV deployment persist in terms of perception noise, viewpoint variations, and control uncertainty."
- **UAV-Flow (B7).** "it remains challenging to conduct systematic real-world experiments and establish consistent evaluation metrics. In future work, we aim to develop a safer and fully closed-loop real-world evaluation framework".
- **HUGE-Bench (B10).** "Future work should investigate stronger domain generalization, online adaptation, and hardware-in-the-loop validation to facilitate reliable real-world deployment."
- **AVDN (B3).** Satellite-image simulation "introduces the shortcoming of having only 2D static scenes".
- **HUGE-Bench (B10).** "The current benchmark primarily focuses on static environments. Incorporating diverse and realistic dynamics, e.g., moving obstacles, changing illumination and weather, or non-stationary objects, remains an important direction".
- **CityNav (B4).** "Simulating other types of disasters and more dynamic scenarios is left for future work". Also: "A promising avenue for future work involves exploring multi-agent collaboration".

### G7. Onboard compute and latency
- **VLA-AN (A6).** Lists "onboard deployment constraints" as a major limitation. "the ARM-based Orin NX offers limited support for the inference of general VLA models, particularly for Vision Transformer (ViT) workloads".
- **Flex (A12).** "computational overhead, which renders it impractical for real-time execution on small mobile robotic platforms."
- **SPF (A10).** "Reactivity to highly dynamic obstacles is limited by the VLM inference latency (≈1-3s)". It lists "reducing system latency for better reactivity, exploring VLM fine-tuning" as future work.
- **SINGER (A9).** "Real-time processing and accurate pose estimation remain challenging to achieve onboard self-sufficient autonomous aerial vehicles, limiting the applicability of VLA policies to UAV navigation".

### G8. Data scarcity and collection cost
- **SINGER (A9).** Open-vocabulary drone navigation "remains an unsolved challenge due to the scarcity of largescale demonstrations".
- **CognitiveDrone (A1).** "The scarcity of standardized, open-source benchmarks and datasets is especially evident in the realm of Unmanned Aerial Vehicles (UAVs), where existing evaluation frameworks are largely confined to racing or basic navigation tasks."
- **VLA-AN (A6).** "Collecting real-world UAV navigation data is labor-intensive; for instance, UAV-FLOW Colosseo [18] spends several weeks to acquire 30K trajectories."
- **OpenFly (B2).** "the collection of VLN data demands extensive human effort to construct trajectories and corresponding instructions, hindering the development of large-scale datasets and capable models."
- **AIR-VLA (A14).** Aerial manipulation VLA "faces a severe 'data scarcity' and an 'absence of benchmarks'".
- **UAV-VLA (A3).** "Future work will focus on creating a specialized dataset for satellite map-based path planning to improve model precision and efficiency in UAV mission generation."

### G9. Perception, grounding, and spatial reasoning
- **SPF (A10).** "VLM inaccuracies (hallucinations and misinterpretations) can occur, and grounding precision may decrease for small or distant targets."
- **AIR-VLA (A14).** "overcoming the critical bottleneck of spatial orientation understanding remains a pivotal challenge".
- **Blukis et al. (A13).** "various problems are not well represented, such as reasoning about obstacles, raising important directions for future work."
- **LANI (B12).** "A potential limitation of our approach is cascading errors."

### G10. Evaluation reproducibility
- **AirNav (B8)** has a section titled "7.4 Reproducibility of Closed-Source Model Results". Its evaluation includes closed-source models such as GPT-4o.
- **UAV-Flow (B7).** "it remains challenging to adopt quantitative evaluation metrics in real-world scenarios."

Not found:
- OpenFly (B2) and IndoorUAV (B9): no explicit limitations section in the extracted text.
- CognitiveDrone (A1): its conclusion gives no limitations.
